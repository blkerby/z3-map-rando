# Logical item placement implementation plan

Status: Steps 1 through 5 and Step 7 implemented. Step 6's global and local state types and item collection are implemented, while traversal initialization remains planned. Steps 8 through 11 remain planned. The placement strategy and the resource, fairy-exchange, and retry policies below reflect the decisions made in discussion.

## Agreed behavior

- All generation uses logical item placement with player tech and proficiency settings.
- Generation must make every catalog item location collectible, including fixed rewards, by executing its collection action rather than merely reaching its associated vertex.
- Save and quit is always available and returns to the catalog's starting vertex while retaining collected inventory and persistent progress. Reset health to the capacity-dependent spawn-health value and clear room-local obstacles. Lose only Blind and Super Bomb; retain Zelda, Old Man, Dwarf, and Purple Chest. Preserve magic, bomb, arrow, and bottle-use counters. Do not require an escape route from a pickup.
- Small and Big Key pickups stay in their vanilla locations. Each small key receives a persistent identity that unlocks exactly one same-dungeon small-key door. Assume the small-key pickup count matches the small-key door count; the updated Ganon's Tower source now has eight small-key pickups and eight small-key doors.
- Represent placeable rewards with a shared `Item` enum containing an explicit variant for each supported pickup, rather than every source receipt or inventory state. Store the reward directly in `ItemPlacement.item`; `Item::SmallKey { door_id: DoorId }` carries the assigned door identity. Persist key assignments within `SeedData.item_placements`, with no parallel assignment array.
- Use `Item::Sword`, `Item::Shield`, `Item::Glove`, `Item::Mail`, and `Item::Boomerang` with no tier payload. Each collected copy advances the corresponding equipment level by one, up to the game's maximum, regardless of collection order. Mail progresses from the starting Green Mail to Blue Mail and then Red Mail; Boomerang progresses from none to Blue and then Red. Model these progressive grants in logic and defer their ROM behavior.
- Use `Item::Ocarina` for the unactivated pickup and track activation as a permanent inventory upgrade from the bird event. Use `Item::SilverArrows` for the arrow upgrade independently of Bow ownership.
- Start without a shield. Two progressive Shield rewards come from the ordinary pool and one from the fixed Waterfall fairy exchange, providing the complete progression from no shield to Blue, Red, and Mirror Shield. The fairy exchange requires at least a Blue Shield and is collected once.
- Assign small-key identities in a preliminary pass, processing one dungeon at a time. Traverse the whole world, following its directed connections, while treating other dungeons' locks and non-key requirements as free. Enforce the selected dungeon's small-key doors and Big Key requirements.
- Assign all unassigned collectible small keys as a batch, so a candidate can open several doors before exposing another key. Do not demand progress from each individual key assignment.
- If a dungeon's preliminary attempt fails, restart only that dungeon. Retain successfully completed assignments for other dungeons. Stop with failure after a bounded number of attempts on a given dungeon; use a constant of 1000.
- During main placement, place a dungeon's Map and Compass in collectible eligible locations before placing any other randomized ordinary rewards there. Keep keys and prizes in their separate location classes.
- Sample candidates from the complete remaining ordinary pool for the newly collectible ordinary locations. There is no separate progression-only fill followed by junk fill.
- A main-placement candidate succeeds when it exposes at least one additional collectible item location, or when all required locations are collectible. Retry failed candidates and restart a failed main-placement attempt without backtracking.
- Treat rupee costs as free. Do not track rupee balances in traversal state.
- Initialize health using the game's capacity-dependent spawn-health table. Magic, bombs, and arrows initially start empty. Once a magic, bomb, or arrow refill is logically obtained, remember that resource in global state so subsequent traversals can start with it full at the current capacity. Health refills do not grant full-health preparation; every traversal starts at spawn health. Resource costs still apply along a route; arriving at another vertex does not replenish resources.
- Only environmental refill sources establish this global preparation permission. Ignore the resource-restoring effects of one-time refill rewards in logical reachability; retain their actual rewards and placement slots. Permanent capacity upgrades still affect logic.
- Track bottles owned and obtainable reusable bottle-content types globally. Local state tracks only `bottles_used`; each use may choose any content known in the traversal's global snapshot and spends one of the bottles available at route initialization. Do not enumerate initial fillings or model bottle refills along a route. Discovering a bottle-content source adds a global preparation flag for subsequent traversals but does not reduce `bottles_used` or enable its content in the current traversal.
- Limit logical bottle contents to Red Potion, Green Potion, Blue Potion, and Fairy. Ignore Bee and Gold Bee contents and refill effects in traversal, including refill discovery. Retain their supported rewards and placement slots.
- Record local resources as amounts used rather than amounts remaining. Track `health_used`, `magic_used`, `bombs_used`, `arrows_used`, and `bottles_used`; derive availability from global capacities. Resource refills raise availability to at least their capacity-capped limit rather than incrementally adding resources; the bottle-use count cannot decrease during a route. This representation supports future traversal-state reuse, while reuse across changes to capacities, preparation, or resource-cost modifiers still needs to account for those changes.
- When defining future cost metrics, prefer Blue Potions over other bottle uses and Red Potions over Fairies. These are cost preferences, not unconditional reachability dominance: retain Fairy revival as an alternative when necessary.
- Keep fixed fairy exchanges in the all-items guarantee. Compile every `shieldExact` requirement as the corresponding minimum-level `shield` requirement; revisit this if fairy-exchange behavior or shield availability changes.
- Migrate the prototype seed representation directly without a format-version update or backward-compatibility machinery.
- Bound candidate retries, per-dungeon key-assignment attempts, and main-placement attempts. Report generation failure when a budget is exhausted.
- Postpone ROM support for door-specific keys and optimization through a reduced preliminary graph.

## Settled resource, fairy-exchange, and retry policies

### Resource preparation

Follow the game's starting-health behavior and start the other resources empty until the traversal after their first logical environmental refill discovery. Thereafter, a global refill-discovery bit permits starting future traversals with that non-health resource full. Store health use relative to full capacity: always initialize `health_used` to health capacity minus spawn health; environmental healing does not grant a preparation flag. Magic, bomb, and arrow use are stored in the one-byte `ResourceUsage(u8)` newtype. `ResourceUsage::UNPREPARED` reserves `u8::MAX` for an initially empty resource that stays empty when capacity increases; ordinary values represent finite depletion relative to full capacity. Prepared full resources start with `ResourceUsage(0)`. Environmental refills and potions preserve the sentinel throughout the traversal; a discovered environmental refill permits starting a later traversal full. Handle the sentinel explicitly rather than applying ordinary arithmetic to it. One-time resource rewards do not establish refill discoveries and their finite amounts are ignored by the logical model, so there is no shared finite-pickup or spending history to track.

Initialize `bottles_used` to zero. Each bottle use requires an owned bottle not yet used on the route and may choose Red Potion, Green Potion, Blue Potion, or Fairy if its global refill flag is established. If no content is known, no bottle use is available. This deliberately abstracts away individual prepared fillings; the only bottle budget is the number owned at route initialization. A bottle-content source encountered on a route contributes a global discovery for a later traversal, without replenishing the current route's bottle budget.

Resource preparation applies at the beginning of a traversal wave. An in-route save-and-quit transition sets `health_used` to health capacity minus spawn health. It preserves `magic_used`, `bombs_used`, `arrows_used`, and `bottles_used`; it does not replenish the current route's resources or bottle budget.

For example, `ThreeBombs`, `TenArrows`, `Heart`, and `SmallMagic` remain rewards in the placement pool but add no logical resource amount. Heart containers, heart pieces, ammunition-capacity upgrades, magic upgrades, and new bottles still increase their permanent capacities; ignore any accompanying one-time refill or initially supplied bottle contents. Only a logically usable reusable bottle-content source establishes the corresponding preparation flag. A Green Potion source encountered on a route discovers Green Potion availability for later traversals; it does not supply an additional bottle use or make Green Potions immediately usable in the current traversal.

Use [`MaxHealthBasedSpawnHP`](jpdasm/bank_09.asm:21348), indexed by maximum whole hearts, for health at the start of each traversal and after save-and-quit. For capacities of 3 through 20 hearts, its starting-heart values are `3, 3, 4, 4, 5, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10`. The ROM table stores these values in eighths of a heart. New-game capacity is three hearts; initial capacities for the other resources follow vanilla defaults and acquired upgrades.

### Fixed fairy exchanges and the all-items guarantee

The current generator keeps the Waterfall and Pyramid fairy exchanges fixed. Preserve those placements and include their locations in the all-items guarantee, converting any sword, shield, or boomerang reward to its progressive variant. The Waterfall boomerang reward contributes one progressive upgrade, collected once after obtaining a boomerang. The Waterfall shield exchange currently compiles to `Never` because exact shield requirements and the free-upgrade helper are disabled. Compile `shieldExact: n` as the same minimum-level shield requirement as `shield: n` everywhere it appears. This makes the exchange's `shieldExact: 1` branch usable without a location-specific override. Progressive shields supersede the earlier rationale that the upgraded shield was obtainable only from this exchange; retain the agreed minimum-level interpretation and revisit it if exact shield levels become necessary for an action.

Retain the current conservative treatment of other exact equipment and negative-flag requirements. Document the general shield interpretation beside its compiler implementation and revisit it if fairy exchanges or shield availability are changed. Do not enable the free-upgrade helper globally or remove the exchange from the required-location set.

### Retry termination

Use separate internal budgets for candidates within a wave, attempts for the current dungeon, and complete main-placement attempts. Candidate exhaustion restarts only the current dungeon in the preliminary phase, or the current main-placement attempt in the main phase. Exhausting the relevant outer budget returns a generation failure through the existing `Result` API. Preserve successfully settled earlier dungeons throughout preliminary retries and preserve all settled key assignments throughout main retries.

Use 1000 attempts per dungeon for preliminary assignment. Choose the other initial budget constants during implementation and tune them with representative generation timings; no new user-facing settings are necessary for the first version. A failure report should identify the phase, selected dungeon when applicable, exhausted budget, and remaining inaccessible locations or lack of progress. Identify cases where mandatory Map/Compass placements consume the available slots, rather than describing every failure as an unlucky ordinary-item sample. Distinguish a failed randomized attempt from a proof that the layout has no solution.

## Current code and scope

[`generator::generate`](crates/generator/src/lib.rs) currently classifies locations, shuffles prizes, places Maps and Compasses within their source dungeon, shuffles the ordinary pool, and constructs `SeedData`. Replace its uniform placement algorithm with logical placement. [`LogicCatalog`](crates/logic_catalog/src/lib.rs) provides authored edges, recursive requirements, effects, typed indices, the start vertex, and separate connection records; it does not implement traversal. [`Settings`](crates/seed_data/src/settings.rs) supplies player tech, proficiencies, and the item pool. [`SeedData`](crates/seed_data/src/output.rs) has no small-key identity assignments.

Implement the first version inside the generator crate, splitting graph preparation, traversal, and placement into modules. Keep the catalog immutable; use catalog indices for generation and stable authored identities for saved seeds. No new crate or general graph framework is needed.

Use the dense `logic_catalog::DungeonIndex` for dungeon ownership. `LogicCatalog.dungeons` maps each dungeon index to its source `RoomIndex` in catalog room order, and each dungeon room has `Room.dungeon_idx: Some(DungeonIndex)`; other rooms, including the flute hub, have `None`. Generation's dungeon inputs use a vector in the same dense order. Compile authored `BigKey` requirements as `Requirement::BigKey(DungeonIndex)` so their scope is explicit rather than inferred when interpreting a generic item requirement. Big Key doors retain their `DoorIndex` requirement and obtain dungeon ownership from their room metadata.

Use vanilla world connections initially. Keep connection expansion separate from traversal so future randomized maps can supply another connection set. Do not implement map randomization, multiple restart destinations, key receipt patches, or door-binding patches in this work.

The logical model will use door-specific keys and progressive swords, shields, gloves, mail, and boomerangs before the ROM does. Saved logical placements can be inspected and solved under that model, but the existing patcher will still produce vanilla key behavior and does not implement progressive equipment receipts. The eventual ROM-playability guarantee depends on the deferred key and progressive-equipment patches and any other gameplay changes assumed by the catalog.

### Step 3: Expand the selected connections into a playable graph

Build outgoing adjacency for catalog edges plus generation-owned connection edges. Expand doors bidirectionally, drops only inward, teleports only outward, and screen boundaries and whirlpools bidirectionally. The destination vertex determines the world; do not add an independent world state to interior vertices.

Inject entrance follower loss as ordinary `LoseFollowers(FollowerMask)` requirements on connection edges. Followers can be discarded whenever needed, so entrance restrictions discard affected followers before entry. Append `ResetObstacles(destination_room_idx)` after departure requirements whenever an edge changes rooms, including flute travel, and on every save-and-quit edge even when its source is already in the starting room. This requirement sets the local obstacle bitmask to zero; its room scope comes from the destination vertex. Match event-exit and event-entry vertices only across the selected physical connection with the same event identity. Do not attach event-entry actions to ordinary endpoint vertices.

Add flute travel using the catalog's destinations and the active-flute capability. Keep save-and-quit behavior available to both traversers. Normal traversal returns to the start with persistent discoveries retained and room obstacles reset, losing only Blind or Super Bomb. Reset health usage to health capacity minus spawn health and retain magic, ammunition, and bottle-use counters. Keep resource preparation at traversal-wave initialization separate from this transition. The simplified traverser can restart directly at the start because it has no resources or followers.

The catalog builder's `build_flute_travel` function appends a synthetic `RoomKind::FluteHub` room containing one ordinary node and vertex, recorded as `LogicCatalog.flute_hub_vertex_idx`, and constructs its travel edges. Keep every vertex's `room_idx` and metadata's `node_idx` mandatory. Catalog rooms use `room_id: Option<SourceRoomId>`: authored rooms have `Some`, and the hub has `None`. The hub has no world, geometry, obstacles, authored strats, items, doors, or physical connection endpoints. It participates in ordinary room-boundary obstacle resets without a special missing-room case.

The builder adds an active-flute edge from every ordinary Light World overworld vertex to the hub, then free edges from the hub to all catalog flute destinations, marking both with `EdgeOrigin::ImplicitFlute`. Exclude interior, Dark World, special-room, and event-entry/exit vertices from departure sources. Flute travel preserves the modeled followers and resources without treating the synthetic room as a cave entrance. The shared hub avoids constructing eight destination edges for every eligible source.

Completion: both traversers can use the same directed world topology, including routes that leave and reenter a dungeon through another entrance.

The normal state, wave-initialization resource preparation, and the two traversers remain in Steps 6 through 8 and Step 4 respectively. The prototype generator remains unchanged until integration in Step 11.

### Step 4: Implement the simplified key-pass traversal

Use mutable traversal state containing visited vertices (`Vec<bool>`), available Small Key doors, Big Key availability, and collected item locations. Initialize an assignment attempt with only the world's start visited. Each traversal call expands the existing visited set using unchanged input inventory and returns only a set of newly collected item locations. The caller records accepted collections and applies inventory updates outside traversal. Initially enqueue every visited vertex and try all outgoing edges; keep the persistent state separate from the work queue so later calls can be optimized to retry only edges affected by new inventory. There is no resource, bottle, follower, or obstacle frontier in this mode.

Evaluate requirement composition while treating non-key leaves as free. Enforce a selected-dungeon small-key door through its candidate assignment. Enforce all selected-dungeon Big Key requirements through collection of its vanilla Big Key, including door locks and explicit `BigKey(DungeonIndex)` requirements on chests or other actions. Treat other dungeons' small-key and Big Key requirements as free. Big chests need no special handling; retaining their Big Key check also supports future changes to key pickup locations. Ignore temporary-state effects; preserve collection discoveries. An unassigned small-key pickup is collectible but enables no door until a candidate assigns it an identity.

Newly collected Small Keys remain unassigned and do not enable doors during traversal. The caller assigns already collected keys and adds their doors to the input state before the next call. The caller also grants the vanilla Big Key from its returned collection location and initiates another traversal to explore newly enabled routes. Traversal does not requeue vertices when it discovers a key and has no outer restart loop. Preserve visited vertices, inventory, and accepted collected item locations in the selected dungeon across calls. Do not seed every dungeon entrance as independently reachable.

Completion: the key pass can ask which target-dungeon keys and items are collectible for a partial candidate assignment.

Implemented in `generator::key_traversal::{KeyPassState, traverse_key_pass}`, using the shared `TraversalGraph` and a selected `DungeonIndex`. `KeyPassState::initialize` seeds only the graph's start; callers can clone the state to isolate candidate trials. Traversal mutates only visited vertices, processes collection effects even on self-loops and already-visited destinations, and returns newly collected item locations in a `hashbrown` set. Collected location records and inventory remain caller-maintained. Explicit Big Key requirements compare dungeon indices directly; door requirements and collection actions resolve dungeon ownership through room metadata. No item-name lookup is needed during traversal. Recursive `And`/`Or` requirements retain selected-dungeon key checks; other leaves, including `Never`, are free in this relaxed pass. Temporary and non-collection effects are ignored. Sort discoveries before using their iteration order to construct randomized candidates. Candidate assignment is implemented in Step 5; generator integration remains in Step 11.

### Step 5: Assign one dungeon's small-key identities at a time

For the current dungeon, start with no assignments and initialized traversal state, then run the simplified traverser. Record returned collections, grant a collected vanilla Big Key outside traversal, and traverse again when that inventory update enables new routes. Gather collectible, unassigned small-key pickups and sample a distinct unassigned door for each pickup in the candidate batch. Clone the traversal state, add the candidate batch's doors to its available doors, and expand reachability, recording candidate collections and applying Big Key updates between calls until closure. Accept the candidate assignments and state if another small key becomes collectible or every item location in that dungeon becomes collectible; otherwise discard them without altering committed assignments or traversal state.

Continue with the newly collectible unassigned keys. Once every dungeon item is collectible, randomly pair any remaining unassigned key pickups and doors. Those keys are already collectible without their new doors, so these final assignments cannot introduce a key-collection cycle. The result assigns an identity to every small-key pickup and door.

If an attempt cannot continue, discard only the current dungeon's assignments and restart that dungeon within its attempt budget. Never discard successfully settled previous dungeons. Other dungeons' locks remain free regardless of their saved assignments. Freeze all successful assignments before main placement.

Completion: every dungeon has a complete key assignment whose keys and item locations are collectible under the preliminary pass's relaxed requirements.

Candidate batches clone traversal state, apply available doors, and record collections and Big Key updates outside traversal. Rejected candidates leave committed assignments and discoveries untouched. Dungeon retries retain previously settled dungeons. Candidate construction follows catalog order before shuffling; hash iteration does not influence RNG calls. The initial budgets are 100 candidates per wave and 1000 attempts per dungeon. Exhaustion reports the dungeon, budgets, last failure reason, and inaccessible locations without claiming that the layout is unsolvable. Generator integration remains in Step 11.

### Step 6: Define normal global and local state and item collection

Global state contains permanent item capabilities and equipment levels, health and resource capacities, heart-piece accumulation, bottles owned, known reusable bottle-content types, discovered environmental magic/bomb/arrow refills, permanent flags, dungeon-scoped Big Keys, and permanently unlocked doors. Store owned Big Keys as a `Vec<bool>` indexed by `DungeonIndex`. Store unlocked doors as a single `Vec<bool>` indexed by `DoorIndex`; Small Key grants and successful door-opening actions set the same bit, without recording the unlocking mechanism. Maps and Compasses have no logical inventory state; retain their placement rules and rewards. Global state separately records reachable item locations and locations whose placed rewards have been collected. One-time refill rewards add no logical resource amount or refill-discovery bit.

Keep the shared reward `common_types::Item` unchanged. Define `logic_catalog::UniqueItem` for boolean ownership capabilities and prizes. `Requirement::Item` carries a `UniqueItem` directly, and `GlobalState::unique_items` is a fixed `[bool; UniqueItem::COUNT]` array indexed by `item as usize`. Collecting a reward sets the corresponding entry directly, without scanning or resolving catalog definitions. Catalog `ItemIndex` and `ItemDefinition` remain only for source reward interpretation and prototype output; their ordering does not affect logical inventory. Half Magic uses a unique-item ownership entry, including for magic-cost scaling. Bottle ownership, Ocarina state, and boomerang levels use dedicated requirements rather than unique-item entries.

Initialize shield level to zero. The first ordinary Shield grants Blue; the Waterfall reward and the other ordinary Shield then grant Red and Mirror in whichever order they are collected. The fairy reward is available once Link has at least shield level one and contributes only one collected upgrade.

Local state contains `health_used`, `magic_used`, `bombs_used`, `arrows_used`, `bottles_used`, a mask of possible followers, and the owning room's obstacle states. Health usage is a `u8` counter; magic, bombs, and arrows use the transparent one-byte `ResourceUsage(u8)` newtype, reserving `u8::MAX` as `ResourceUsage::UNPREPARED`. Store capacities as `u8` as well: maximum health is 160 eighths, magic capacity is 128, bomb capacity is 50, and arrow capacity is 70. Half Magic reduces costs rather than doubling stored magic capacity. For each `UseMagic` requirement, halve cumulative usage and round up conservatively; separate requirements round separately. Keep per-use potion opportunities, and do not model Cape timer phase or fractional magic. Keep catalog quantities and intermediate resource calculations wider, and store only bounded usage values. Finite usage represents net depletion from full capacity, so damage and spending increase it while healing and resource refills decrease it, bounded below by zero. Full health has zero used and prepared full resources use `ResourceUsage(0)`; initially empty magic and ammunition use `ResourceUsage::UNPREPARED` so capacity upgrades do not create resources, and remain unprepared throughout the traversal even after refills or potion use. Health must remain positive unless a Fairy revival succeeds. Omit rupees. Keep the entire vector of resource-use counters together in actual route states rather than taking independent minima across different routes.

Store `cleared_obstacles` as a `u8` bitmask, where bit n corresponds to `ObstacleIndex(n)` in the room of the vertex holding this local state. Do not store a room index in `LocalState`; room ownership is implicit in the containing vertex. The current source has at most four obstacles in a room, in Swamp Palace. Resetting room-local obstacles sets the mask to zero.

Use the transparent one-byte `FollowerMask(u8)` type for both `LocalState::possible_followers` and `Requirement::LoseFollowers`. Store `possible_followers` as a bitmask representing alternative single-follower states. Bit `follower as u8` corresponds to a catalog `Follower` (Zelda, Old Man, Blind, Dwarf, Purple Chest, or Super Bomb). No follower needs no dedicated bit because any follower can be freely discarded whenever needed. Initialize the mask to zero, meaning no follower is possible; this is a valid state. A follower requirement fails when its bit is absent and otherwise retains only the required follower's bit; alternatives cannot satisfy successive requirements for different followers without an intervening follower-changing action. `SetFollower(Some(follower))` replaces the mask with that follower's bit, and `SetFollower(None)` sets the mask to zero. `LoseFollowers` clears the bits selected by its mask, preserving unselected possibilities and succeeding even when the result is zero. When other local state matches, follower alternatives can be merged by unioning their masks; multiple set bits never mean simultaneous companions.

Bottle availability consists of global Red Potion, Green Potion, Blue Potion, and Fairy refill flags and the owned bottle count. Local state has no concrete contents or empty-bottle count: consuming any known content increments the shared `bottles_used` count. With four bottles, this component has only five possible values, zero through four, regardless of the number of known content types. Bee and Gold Bee rewards and refill sources add no logical contents or preparation permissions.

Implement item collection through exhaustive matches on the shared `Item` enum. Each `Item::Sword`, `Item::Shield`, `Item::Glove`, `Item::Mail`, or `Item::Boomerang` increments its equipment level by one up to the game's maximum, independently of the source tier or pickup order. The two Glove pickups grant Power Glove and Titan's Mitt capabilities in that order. Initialize mail level to zero for Green Mail; the two Mail pickups grant Blue Mail and then Red Mail, capped at level two. Use the acquired mail level for the catalog's damage table. Initialize boomerang level to zero; its ordinary pickup grants Blue and the fixed fairy reward grants Red, capped at level two. Source Blue Boomerang requirements accept level one or higher, while Red Boomerang requirements require level two. `Item::SilverArrows` grants only the silver-arrow capability; shooting still requires Bow ownership and ammunition. Capacities derive from acquired upgrades, and every fourth Piece of Heart increases health capacity directly without an internal receipt reward. Collect each location once. Collect a Small Key by resolving its `Item::SmallKey` door payload through `GenerationInputs::door_indices`, a `hashbrown::HashMap<DoorId, DoorIndex>`, and setting the corresponding `unlocked_doors` bit. Build this reverse map from catalog doors during input preparation, before key assignment; assignments choose the door identity stored at each key location without changing the identity-to-index mapping. Resolve Big Key scope from the pickup's dungeon. Keep logic-catalog item requirements typed as `UniqueItem` rather than replacing them with placement-specific rewards, evaluating equipment requirements against acquired progressive levels.

Represent flute equipment as `OcarinaState::{Missing, Inactive, Active}` in `logic_catalog`. `Item::Ocarina` raises the state to at least `Inactive` while preserving an already active flute; duplicate pickups do not activate it. The bird event uses `Effect::ActivateOcarina` to set it to `Active`. `Requirement::Ocarina` carries the minimum required state: source `OcarinaInactive` requirements accept either owned state, while `OcarinaActive` requirements require `Active`. Activation remains separate from placed rewards.

Use eighths of a heart for health and the catalog's normalized magic units. Each potion use requires the corresponding global refill flag and an unused bottle, then increments `bottles_used`. A Red Potion sets `health_used` to zero, a Green Potion sets `magic_used` to zero, and a Blue Potion sets both to zero. With the Fairy flag and an unused bottle, Fairy revival after a fatal hit increments `bottles_used` and sets `health_used` to health capacity minus seven hearts, with revival health capped by capacity. Distinguish a one-time filled-bottle reward from a reusable refill source; buying or finding one potion does not itself establish a preparation flag.

Completion: applying a reward produces the capacities and capabilities needed by requirement evaluation. Route initialization follows the spawn-health table and known environmental refill bits.

State types are defined in `generator::state`: `GlobalState` holds a fixed array of unique-item ownership entries, including Half Magic, progressive equipment levels, Ocarina state, capacities, refill flags, dungeon-scoped Big Keys, a unified vector of unlocked doors, progress flags, and separate `Vec<bool>` records for reachable and collected item locations indexed by `ItemLocationIndex`. Reachable locations have executable collection actions, including unfilled slots; collected locations have had their placed rewards applied. It excludes Map/Compass ownership. `LocalState` holds resource usage, the shared bottle-use count, a follower-possibility mask, and room-scoped obstacles. `ResourceUsage` uses a one-byte sentinel representation to distinguish initially empty resources from finite depletion, and `RefillFlags` includes only the four supported bottle-content types. `GlobalState::collect_item` exhaustively handles typed rewards, caps equipment and capacity upgrades, accumulates heart pieces, preserves Ocarina activation, and grants dungeon-scoped Big Keys and door-specific Small Keys. ### Step 7: Interpret normal requirements and effects

Implement each `Requirement` variant over a fixed global snapshot and a bounded batch of local input states. Execute `And` children in order; evaluate each `Or` branch from the same input and retain meaningful successful output states. Honor tech enablement, proficiency thresholds, prize counts, followers, obstacles, and doors. `LoseFollowers` always succeeds, clearing the bits selected by its `FollowerMask` while preserving all unselected possibilities; a zero mask remains a valid state. `ResetObstacles(room_idx)` sets the local obstacle bitmask to zero, without changing persistent door openings or other global progress; room ownership is determined by the vertex holding the resulting local state. Interpret injected connection requirements through the same interpreter as authored requirements. Preserve `Never` in normal traversal.

`Requirement::Door(door_idx)` checks permanent access only in the global snapshot, never in pending discoveries. It succeeds when the corresponding `unlocked_doors` bit is set. An otherwise locked Big Key door checks the owning dungeon's Big Key and immediately reports a permanent unlock when that requirement succeeds, even if a later requirement fails. Small Key doors require the bit supplied by their assigned key. All other lock types require a prior `OpenDoor` effect; the authored unlocking strat supplies the actual requirements and costs. A successful `OpenDoor(door_idx)` effect discovers the same permanent unlock bit that a door-specific Small Key grant supplies. Global state records permission to pass the door without distinguishing how that permission was obtained.

Apply ammunition and magic costs, damage, refills, and resource-state checks to the local resource-use counters. Costs increase usage. Health refills and prepared magic, bomb, and arrow refills raise availability to `max(current, min(limit, capacity))`, equivalently setting usage to `min(used, capacity - min(limit, capacity))`, without accumulating resources across repeated applications. Leave unprepared resources unchanged when applying this floor. Non-health refills also discover full-resource preparation for later traversal waves; health refills do not. Revisiting a source after spending resources may still restore the same floor; this is the accepted compromise for source data that cannot express depletion and respawn requirements. Compare usage with global capacities to determine whether a route can pay a cost. Batch magic uses up to the next required potion, preserving per-use boundaries and cumulative Half Magic rounding; the loop needs at most one additional iteration per bottle consumed. Process damage hits individually. Use current resources first, and consider potions only when the next magic use, damage hit, or resource-state check cannot succeed. For magic-use costs, use Blue if available, otherwise Green; Blue restores the same magic and also heals for the same bottle cost. For damage, use Blue if available, otherwise Red; Blue restores the same health and also refills magic for the same bottle cost. Try Blue/Green for a failing magic-state check and Blue/Red for a failing health-state check; do not enumerate potion uses on ordinary edges or already-satisfied requirements. Keep Fairy revival as an alternative when a hit would be fatal, including hits that a potion cannot make survivable. A bottle use checks its content's global refill flag and increments `bottles_used`, without selecting or storing concrete contents. Bottle-content refill operations only discover preparation flags for later traversals; they never decrease `bottles_used` or supply additional current-route uses. A Fairy source immediately raises health to at least seven hearts per available Fairy, capped by health capacity. Establishing the Fairy bottle-preparation flag requires a bottle and the Bug-Catching Net. Do not reserve a Fairy or branch between healing and capture: the global preparation discovery can represent capture on a previous route. Rupee payment and refill operations have no balance effect.

Report non-health environmental refill discoveries and Big Key door openings immediately when their requirement operations succeed. Retain those global discoveries if a later `And` child or the rest of an `Or` branch fails; never discover an operation behind an unsatisfied earlier requirement. Local resource updates still belong to successful output states, so failed paths do not produce a destination state. Accumulate discoveries separately from local states, and apply them between traversal waves even when an edge returns no successful local states. Newly discovered bottle contents remain unavailable within the current wave and never restore its bottle budget. `Requirement::make_and` must preserve `Never` in sequence so earlier operations can report discoveries. `Requirement::make_or` omits bare `Never` branches but preserves `Free` alongside state-changing alternatives.

Apply strat effects only after the whole requirement succeeds. Temporary obstacle and follower effects update the local state. Collection, fixed item grants, flags, and persistent door openings become global discoveries only through successful actions; a failed suffix must not grant them. Do not grant a hypothetical item from an unfilled location: report that the collection action is executable so the filler can use the slot.

Implemented in `generator::interpreter::Interpreter::evaluate_edge`, taking a `LocalStateReducer` of source states and returning `EdgeResult` with successful local states and `GlobalDiscoveries`. The interpreter borrows immutable catalog, generation inputs, and global inventory. Requirement evaluation takes and returns a `LocalStateReducer`: `And` chains whole batches through its children, while each `Or` branch starts from a clone of the same input batch. Passing state-independent checks move the input reducer unchanged. Local requirements use a single-state helper that inserts successful outputs into a shared reducer; magic batching keeps resource progress separate for each input. Reached global discoveries accumulate independently of whether the whole action succeeds. Potion choices use only the global snapshot and share the owned-bottle budget. They are evaluated on demand for an insufficient resource, including between magic uses or damage hits and for a failing health/magic resource-state check; already-satisfied operations leave bottles untouched. Magic evaluation computes the last affordable use directly and advances to the next potion point rather than iterating over every use. At each magic-use deficit, use only the first available potion in Blue/Green order, since Green cannot improve on Blue at the same bottle cost. For a fatal damage hit that a potion can make survivable, use only the first available potion in Blue/Red order, while retaining Fairy revival as an independent alternative. Fairy sources apply a local health floor and independently discover preparation when a bottle and the Bug-Catching Net are available, without reserving a Fairy for capture. Successful strat effects report item collection actions, activation, flags, and persistent doors without applying inventory. Discoveries exclude progress already recorded in the global snapshot, including reachable item locations; a newly reachable location is still reported even if its reward capability is already owned. Item discovery filtering uses `reachable_item_locations`, independently of `collected_item_locations`, so unfilled locations are not rediscovered in later closure waves. Known refill sources still update prepared local resource usage without reporting another global discovery. Environmental refills and potions preserve unprepared magic and ammunition; magic-use and missing-magic checks cannot use potions to bypass this preparation boundary. Follower and obstacle effects update returned local states. Save-and-quit edges include `Requirement::ResetHealthToSpawn`, evaluated through `GlobalState::get_spawn_health` while retaining magic, ammunition, and bottle use. `evaluate_edge` does not interpret edge origins. Health missing-resource thresholds are compiled to eighths of a heart, matching refill units. `EdgeResult::local_states` and intermediate requirement collections use `LocalStateReducer`, retaining the actual winners of 18 fixed cost metrics and keeping incumbents on ties. Reduction occurs between `And` children, when merging `Or` alternatives, between potion/damage steps, and after edge effects. Discoveries already recorded by evaluated paths survive removal of their local states. The metric bound is an intentional completeness approximation; normal traversal and inventory closure remain in Step 8.

### Step 8: Traverse normal routes and compute discovery closure

Use a work queue of vertex/local-state pairs. Keep separate reachable and collected item-location records in `GlobalState`. Record newly reachable collection actions between waves, including unfilled slots, so later waves report only newly reachable locations. Apply assigned rewards from reachable, uncollected locations through `GlobalState::collect_item`, including rewards placed into previously reachable slots. Only reward application marks a location collected and duplicate application returns an error. Store multiple compatible local states at a vertex. Begin with exact deduplication and conservative resource dominance between states with matching temporary state: a state using no more health, magic, bombs, arrows, or bottles can dominate another under the same global snapshot. Retain alternatives with different obstacle or follower states or tradeoffs among resource-use counters. Never combine unrelated states into an invented route. Resource-use counters prepare the representation for future traversal-state reuse, but implementing that optimization remains deferred.

When cost metrics are introduced, prefer Blue Potions over other bottle uses and Red Potions over Fairies. Keep these preferences separate from safe reachability pruning: a Fairy can revive after a hit exceeding maximum health, while a potion used beforehand cannot prevent that death. Evaluate Fairy revival when required rather than replacing it with potion healing. Defer metric-limited frontier pruning until its completeness tradeoff is explicitly addressed.

Run a traversal wave with a fixed global snapshot, gather discoveries, apply the guaranteed discoveries, and repeat until no additional global capabilities, flags, door openings, or refill types are discovered. Global discoveries may come from fixed placements, committed randomized placements, fixed events, or refill operations reached through successful requirement prefixes, including edges with no successful destination state. Each new traversal initializes known refilled non-health resources with zero usage, initially empty magic and ammunition with `ResourceUsage::UNPREPARED`, and health usage from the spawn-health table. Initialize `bottles_used` to zero and make only the global snapshot's known bottle-content types available. Do not rerun preparation when following a save-and-quit edge within a wave; reset health usage and selectively lose followers and obstacles while preserving the other use counters.

Keep inventory dependent continuations for the next wave; a successful collection effect does not let the old snapshot immediately use the new item. Track the provenance of discoveries and successful collection actions sufficiently to explain inaccessible locations during development.

Return the collectible location set and executable unfilled collection actions. Do not use vertex reachability as a substitute for collection. Postpone a bounded metric frontier until measurements show a need; adding one would require an explicit decision about lost completeness.

Keep adjacency order, work-queue processing, requirement alternatives, discovery application, and state tie handling deterministic. Sort unordered collections before they affect candidate construction or RNG calls. Stable saved output alone does not make generation reproducible if RNG sampling depends on hash iteration order.

Completion: given a partial placement, the filler can obtain all currently collectible slots and permanent discoveries under normal logic.

### Step 9: Prepare main placement and apply Map/Compass priority

Start each main attempt with the frozen small-key assignments, fixed vanilla key rewards, fixed fairy rewards, and a fresh shuffle of pendants and crystals among prize locations. Preassign prizes using the current placement restriction, then let normal traversal collect them when their boss reward action is executable. Do not assume possession of a prize merely because it is assigned.

Traverse committed placements to closure. For newly collectible eligible ordinary locations in each dungeon, place that dungeon's outstanding Map and Compass first, choosing their slots with the RNG. If only one slot is collectible, place one and carry the other forward. Ordinary pool rewards may use that dungeon's remaining collectible slots only once both dungeon items are placed.

Treat these forced dungeon-item placements and the sampled ordinary rewards as the complete candidate for the wave. Account for all pool quantities without pre-reserving arbitrary inaccessible dungeon slots.

Preserve the hard Map/Compass priority even when it consumes every currently available slot in a dungeon. If it prevents progress, use the agreed retry policy and report the forced-placement bottleneck; do not silently relax the rule.

Completion: candidate construction honors Map/Compass priority, fixed-key locations, and prize-only placement restrictions.

### Step 10: Sample, accept, and retry placement waves

Sample without replacement from the remaining ordinary pool to fill every other newly collectible ordinary slot. Order eligible locations and candidate inputs deterministically before sampling. Evaluate the full candidate with normal traversal to closure; items that only help as a combination can therefore succeed together.

Accept the candidate when its collectible-location set includes at least one previously uncollectible required item location, or when all required locations are collectible. On acceptance, commit its placements and pool removals. On rejection, leave the committed placements, remaining pool, and global state unchanged, then sample another candidate. Advance RNG state normally so retries do not repeat the same candidate.

Reaching all locations does not end placement if some remain unfilled. Once every required location is collectible, fill the remaining eligible slots from their remaining pools, respecting Map/Compass priority and prize restrictions. This final fill needs no further progress gain. Finish only when every location has a reward, all pools are consumed, and the completed placement remains fully collectible in the final solve.

If the attempt cannot continue, restart main placement with its ordinary placements and prize shuffle cleared and its Map/Compass pools restored. Retain the settled dungeon key assignments. No placement backtracking is needed. Enforce the agreed candidate and outer-attempt budgets and report failure when the outer budget is exhausted.

Completion: the generator can construct a complete logical assignment through advancing waves and restarts.
