# Seed corpus test

Status: planned.

A test reads a committed corpus of saved seeds with current code and catalogs, to verify that seeds from every release remain readable and are interpreted as they were generated. It complements the [seed vocabulary test](../crates/catalog_builder/tests/seed_vocabulary.rs): that test covers every saved identifier, including ones no seed uses yet, but not the seed format or its interpretation; the corpus covers only what its seeds contain, but exercises the whole reading path.

## Corpus

Plain seed JSON files under `data/seed_corpus/`, about 25 KB each. Each release adds seeds generated by that release, chosen so that every saved field shape occurs: vanilla and random layouts, an item pool smaller than the location count (empty slots), starting equipment, and different skill presets. After release, corpus files are never edited or removed. Before the first release, the corpus is regenerated whenever the seed format changes intentionally.

## Test

For each corpus seed:

1. Deserialize it strictly with current `seed_data` (see [reading seed records](../docs/data-formats/seed-data.md#reading-seed-records)).
2. Migrate and resolve its settings, once [settings migration](../docs/data-formats/settings.md#versioning-and-migration) exists.
3. Map its rooms, placements, and Small Key doors to current catalog entries, rebuild its `WorldLayout`, and build its connections ([spoiler](spoiler.md) work items 1 and 2).
4. Replay its spheres ([spoiler](spoiler.md) work item 3) and require every placement to be reachable.
5. Prepare its patch. This needs the patch catalog, which CI does not build yet because it requires Asar.

The test is most useful once the spoiler replay exists; steps 1 and 3 could be added earlier.

## Open questions

- Whether a logic change that makes a corpus placement unreachable is always a failure, or may be accepted after review, given that the spoiler treats unreachable placements as a reportable outcome.
- Whether to check more than success in step 5, such as comparing prepared patch data against a recorded result.
