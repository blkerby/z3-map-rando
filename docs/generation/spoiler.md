# Spoiler

Seed pages have a collapsed "Spoiler" section showing one row of item icons per sphere, with each item's location on hover.

## Approach

The spoiler is not stored with the seed. It is an exploration of the seed, defined by its world layout and item placements, against the current logic. The browser computes it on demand by replaying logical traversal through WebAssembly, using the current logic catalog and the seed's resolved settings. Seeds therefore need no spoiler data and no compatibility rules for it, and the spoiler is independent of the generator's placement algorithm.

Because current logic may differ from the logic that generated the seed, some placements may be unreachable in the replay, or reachable in other spheres than at generation. Unreachable locations are listed in a final group rather than treated as errors. A saved room, item location, or Small Key door missing from the current catalog is an error that stops the replay; the [seed vocabulary](../../crates/catalog_builder/tests/seed_vocabulary.rs) test keeps released identifiers from disappearing.

## Spheres

Spheres are classic: sphere 1 is everything collectible with the starting inventory, and sphere N is everything newly collectible with the items of all earlier spheres. Every item, including Small Keys, Big Keys, and prizes, waits for the next sphere to take effect. Non-item discoveries, such as flags, door openings with already-owned keys, Ocarina activation, and refills, are applied within a sphere until no more are found, since they need no item pickup.

[`generator::spoiler::replay_spoiler`](../../crates/generator/src/spoiler.rs) rebuilds the seed's `WorldLayout` and traversal graph, maps each saved `(room_id, item_id)` to a catalog item location, and reuses `compute_closure` (see [traversal](traversal.md)), which collects only locations that have a placement. Each call has only the earlier spheres' placements filled in, so it explores and applies non-item discoveries but collects nothing new. The newly reachable locations form the next sphere; their placements are filled in for the following call, which collects them at its start and explores further. The replay ends when a call discovers no new location. Traversal state carries over between calls, so each call revisits only the edges affected by newly collected items. For tracing routes, the replay keeps a copy of the global state after each call that yields a sphere. Through the per-wave callback of `compute_closure`, it also keeps the global state of each wave that first discovers a flag, door, or Ocarina activation, before that wave's discoveries are applied.

## Routes

A route is traced on demand by `SpoilerReplay::trace_route`, as a fresh search rather than from the replay's incremental traversals, which can take roundabout paths. Its target is an item location to collect, or a flag, door, or Ocarina activation to set by a strat effect. The search holds a saved global state fixed and applies no discoveries:

- For an item location, the state of its sphere: the items of all earlier spheres, with every flag, door, Ocarina activation, and refill they allow. Nothing in it depends on the target.
- For a discovery, the state of the wave that first discovered it: the progress of earlier waves, without the discovery itself or anything that depends on it. A route's own dependencies were therefore discovered in earlier waves, so following them never cycles.

Small Key and Big Key doors are unlocked by owning their key rather than by a strat, so `SpoilerReplay::resolve_door` maps them to their key's location. Progress held from the start, such as the `GameStateZelda` flag, has no route. Routes start at Link's House.

The search is Dijkstra's algorithm over pairs of a vertex and a local state, with each edge costing 1, so it finds a route with the fewest edges. Each entry records the entry and edge it was reached from. A per-vertex `LocalStateReducer` decides which states are worth expanding, as in normal traversal; the reducer is lossy and order-dependent, so a found route may not be strictly minimal, and in rare cases no route may be found for a location the replay reached. An entry whose edge collects or sets the target is queued even if its reducer rejects it, and popping it ends the search. Edges are evaluated one source state at a time through the ordinary `Interpreter`, so route tracing adds no bookkeeping to generation; generation passes a no-op wave callback, which compiles away.

## Browser and page

The browser package exports a `SpoilerSession` class, which replays the seed JSON against the logic catalog (using the seed's recorded settings or optional override settings when unlocked on the seed page) and keeps the replay in WebAssembly memory. Its `replay` method recomputes spheres and discovery states under updated settings without rebuilding the seed's graph or reloading the world maps. Its `get_spheres` returns the spheres and unreachable locations as JSON. Each entry has the catalog location index, the location name, the item kind token, the name of the door a Small Key opens, and the dungeon name of a Map, Compass, or Big Key. Its `trace_route` takes a target as JSON, such as `{"kind": "location", "index": 3}` or `{"kind": "ocarina"}`, and returns the route as JSON rows, or null if none is found. The first row is the route's start; each later row is one edge, with the name of the node it reaches, whether it returns to the node it leaves from, whether that node is in the Dark World, the strat name unless it is exactly "Base", the flags, doors, and Ocarina activation that the edge's requirement reads (including alternatives the route may not have used), each with the target of its own route if it has one, and the local state after the edge. The edges into and out of the synthetic flute hub form one row. A row that enters a room or changes world names the connection and the room entered, such as "Start at Link's House", "Cross West to Swamp Ruins", "Enter Ice Palace", or "Flute to Magic Shop"; a strat that changes world, such as a Dark World portal, names only the room. Overworld rooms use their Dark World names in the Dark World. An interior room's world is that of the overworld side of its entrances in the seed, all of which lead to the same world. The spoiler adds about 72 KB gzipped to the package. A replay takes about 70–80 ms in the browser, and a route a few milliseconds.

The seed page fetches the logic catalog (about 55 KB) and runs the replay the first time the section is opened ([`SpoilerList.svelte`](../../frontend/src/lib/spoiler/SpoilerList.svelte)). Each sphere is a row of icons, one per item so that each stays individually hoverable, ordered by placement tier with prizes and dungeon items between the tiers; empty slots are omitted. Progressive items show the level each copy reaches, counting copies from the seed's starting equipment in sphere order and then catalog order. Items without an icon yet show a text badge until an icon named after the item is added to the item assets.

Rows start hidden as rows of question marks, and clicking a hidden row reveals it and every row above it; buttons reveal or hide all rows. Clicking a revealed item shows its route in a dialog up to 70rem wide and nearly the window's height: the route scrolls on the left, and the map stays in place on the right, sized to the dialog's height while leaving the route at least 20rem; on narrow screens, the map sits above the route instead. The route is grouped into runs of rows, each starting at a row that enters a room or changes world and headed by its description. Groups start collapsed, and their rows are indented under the heading; Dark World groups have a darker background. A group's first row reads "Start at <node>"; a strat that changes world, such as a Dark World portal, instead heads its group as "<strat> to <room>", where clicking the strat's link opens it without toggling the group. Later rows read "<strat> to <node>", "Go to <node>" for a "Base" strat, or just the strat name, or "Base", for a strat returning to the node it leaves from. The strat name, or the node for "Go to", links to the strat's logic page. Below each row, indented and muted, come the dependencies and the state components that differ from the previous row, with icons for health, magic, bombs, arrows, and bottles. Each dependency with a route is a link that replaces the dialog's route with its own. The map is the seed's Light World Mode 7 map, with half of the clouds around the room grid cropped away. The session's `load_world_maps` compiles both maps from the player's vanilla ROM, the seed's filtered retiling catalog, and the patch catalog, the same inputs as patching, and returns them as PNGs. The maps render once, the first time a route is shown if a ROM is saved, or else from a button that asks for one.

The route is drawn over the map as lines between points where it crosses connections, since logic nodes have no coordinates. A map boundary crossing has points 2 map pixels before and after the center of the source node's opening on the side crossed, at the room's position in the seed's layout. The opening comes from the edge variant the seed selects for each segment and the standard variant geometry in `patcher::retiling::get_edge_openings`; for a specialized variant without specified geometry, or a vanilla layout, which selects no variants, the segment's middle is used instead. Only segments facing the room of the crossing's other endpoint count, and a `light-and-mirror` crossing uses the ordinary Light World role, or for a Mirror crossing the Dark World Mirror spot, so a node listed in several segments or roles gets the opening the crossing actually uses. Where the source node still has several openings, such as both of a `dual-small` variant's, the first is used. The line between the two points shows the direction of the crossing; a route leaving and returning has only the first. Other points are placed as follows: a door at its tile from the ROM's entrance table, which `load_world_maps` reads, a pit at its hole's center from a hard-coded list (the ROM's pit table stores a camera-relative position instead; see [entrances](../engine/entrances.md)), and a special area's entrance at the middle of its vanilla transition tiles; the dialog traces the shown route again once they are loaded. The last point of a route to an item that sits on the overworld is the item's position, hard-coded from vanilla sprite lists and tile checks. Each route row carries its points and whether it is a warp or starts in an interior. Lines take the world of their starting point: travel in the shown world is solid, travel in the other world is dashed and dimmed, and warps and interior travel are dotted. Each line animates in the direction of travel, regardless of reduced-motion preferences, since the motion is what shows the direction. Link's head marks the start, and a star the last point. The page frees the session when the seed page is left.
